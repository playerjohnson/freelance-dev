Why Hire a Freelance .NET Developer in 2026

A freelance .NET developer can provide specialist support for a defined project or an existing team. Compare that option with agency delivery and permanent hiring against your scope, budget and long-term support needs.

Compare Total Cost

Compare written proposals against the same scope and deliverables. Include onboarding, delivery, hosting, maintenance, support and handover where they apply. A day rate alone does not establish the total cost or value of an engagement.

For a permanent hire, consider employment costs, recruitment and onboarding alongside the long-term capacity you need. For an agency or freelancer, confirm who will deliver the work, what the quote includes and how changes will be agreed.

Agree a Start Date

Availability depends on current commitments, recruitment or procurement, and the access needed to begin. Confirm capacity, scope and a start date before making delivery plans. Contact me to discuss availability.

Find Relevant Expertise

Match the developer's experience to the work you need, whether that is .NET application development, Azure migration, integration or technical leadership. Ask how they would approach the constraints of your project and work with your existing team.

For public sector work, discuss the organisation's procurement, security, accessibility and service-governance requirements. Agree who owns each decision and what evidence will be needed.

Manage Delivery Risk

Agree scope, milestones, acceptance criteria, communication and handover in writing. Review progress and changes regularly, and understand the payment and termination provisions before committing.

Delivered work includes a 30-day defect correction period from delivery for bugs directly attributable to that work. Payment and termination follow the agreed written terms.

Plan Onboarding and Handover

Allow for the time needed to understand the codebase, business rules, access arrangements and delivery process. Agree an initial assessment and realistic milestones rather than assuming immediate productivity.

Expect clear communication about progress, blockers and decisions. Include documentation and knowledge transfer in the scope so your team can understand the delivered work and plan ongoing maintenance.

Assess Supporting Evidence

Ask about relevant examples and the person's role in each one. Keep employed work, personal products and freelance commissions distinct. Supporting material and references should only be shared where permission allows.

A portfolio or technical article can help you understand someone's approach, but it does not independently verify a project outcome. Discuss the evidence behind any claimed result and check permitted references where useful.

If an initial assessment or paid trial would help, agree its scope, price, deliverables and exit terms in writing. It should form part of the agreed engagement rather than an assumed offer.

Choose the Right Engagement

A permanent role may suit an ongoing need for continuity and organisational knowledge. An agency may suit delivery that needs a coordinated team. A freelancer may suit a defined project or specialist support alongside your existing team.

If the scope is unclear, consider a discovery phase to understand the problem, constraints and options before committing to delivery. Choose the engagement around those needs and the written proposal.
